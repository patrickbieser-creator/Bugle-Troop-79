High Adventure Trip Recap

 

Two Crews, 60 Miles, and Not a Single Fish

Two crews of Troop 79's older scouts are back from their weeklong canoe trip through the Boundary Waters Canoe Area Wilderness with stories of a week of beautiful weather, canoe portages, improved paddling skills, remarkable wildlife, and absolutely no fish.

There were nibbles. There were tugs. Several submerged logs put up a respectable fight. But despite our best efforts, no actual fish were caught. Perhaps the wind was blowing from the wrong direction? Or it was too hot? Or, our hats were the wrong color? We only have working theories.

Crew One began at Entry Point 30 on Lake One and took a more modest route, traveling approximately 20 miles and completing eight portages. Crew Two, made up of the older Scouts, entered at Snowbank Lake and covered nearly 40 miles, including more than a mile and a half of portaging.

The weather could hardly have been better. Most days were sunny and in the 70s, with little wind to interfere with paddling or portaging. Crew One traveled through rain on Wednesday, while the older Scouts wisely remained at camp. The rain was welcome, however. Fire restrictions in the BWCA had ended just before our trip and have just been re-imposed, so we were fortunate to arrive during a well-timed stretch of wetter, safer conditions.

The Boundary Waters covers more than a million acres, but somehow our two crews managed to find one another on the final day. Completely by accident, they selected campsites only about half a mile apart within roughly 100 square miles of wilderness.

Along the way, the scouts saw beavers, cranes, a turtle laying eggs, swans, ducks, and even a moose wandering through the night. After sunset, the sky opened into a brilliant field of stars -- darker, clearer, and more expansive than many of us had ever seen.

The food was good (for being freeze dried). The Duluth packs were heavy. The mosquitoes appeared faithfully every evening but remained manageable with enough repellent and a few head nets. Everyone became a stronger paddler, learned to work more efficiently as a crew, and gained a new appreciation for the extraordinary beauty of the wilderness. We came home with stronger skills, a life time of stories, and memories that will last much longer than any fish would have.


Troop HUGE Rummage Sale

 

Saturday, August 29, 2026, 8:00am - 6:00pm
Uniform: Class A Uniform
Location: Northwoods Parking Lot
Food Brat Sale

Troop 79 is planning a large community rummage sale to raise money for troop programs, camping, equipment and activities. We are inviting troop families, friends, neighbors and members of the community to donate useful items. This will be a major troop-wide project, and its success will depend on everyone helping with donations, promotion, setup, staffing and cleanup.

Our goal is to raise approximately $5,000 or more for Troop 79.

A successful sale could help support:

  • Camping trips and outdoor activities
  • Troop equipment and supplies
  • Advancement and leadership programs
  • Financial assistance for Scouts
  • Future troop events and special opportunities

The more high-quality donations we receive, and the more people that attend, the more successful the sale will be.

What We Are Looking For

We are looking for clean, useful, complete and saleable items in good condition. Popular rummage-sale items include:

  • Bicycles and sporting goods
  • Tools and lawn or garden equipment
  • Camping and outdoor gear
  • Toys, games and LEGO
  • Books, puzzles, movies and music
  • Kitchenware and small appliances
  • Home decor and household goods
  • Lamps and small furniture
  • Electronics that are current and working
  • Brand name clothing, shoes and accessories
  • Collectibles and hobby items
  • School, office and craft supplies

Items should be clean and ready to sell. Electronics and appliances should be tested whenever possible.

Items We Cannot Accept

Please do not donate items that are broken, dirty, unsafe, incomplete or unlikely to sell. We cannot accept:

  • Mattresses or box springs
  • Cribs, car seats or other used child-safety equipment
  • Paint, chemicals, fuel, propane tanks or hazardous materials
  • Tires, automotive batteries or automotive fluids
  • Firearms, ammunition or other weapons
  • Old tube televisions or console televisions
  • Broken appliances or electronics
  • Large exercise machines
  • Used medical equipment
  • Opened toiletries, cosmetics or personal-care products
  • Moldy, stained, wet, smoke-damaged or pet-soiled items
  • Recalled products
  • Furniture with major damage or missing parts

The troop reserves the right to decline donations that cannot be safely or reasonably sold. Please remember that anything left after the sale must be donated, recycled or discarded. Unusable donations create additional work and disposal costs for the troop.

What We Need From Scout Families

Begin setting aside quality items now. Ask relatives, friends, neighbors and coworkers whether they have items they would like to contribute. Please focus on items that you would feel comfortable purchasing yourself.

Families may be asked to help transport donations from homes to Northwoods. Scouts and adults with trucks, vans or trailers will be especially helpful. Do not collect large furniture or unusually heavy items without first confirming that the troop can accept and transport them.

We need every family to help spread the word. Please share the sale through:

  • Neighborhood social-media groups
  • Facebook and community pages
  • School, church and workplace networks
  • Friends, relatives and neighbors
  • Yard signs and printed flyers
  • Email and text-message groups

Strong attendance will be just as important as the volume of donations.

We will need Scouts and adults to help with:

  • Receiving and sorting donations
  • Testing and cleaning merchandise
  • Pricing items
  • Setting up tables and displays
  • Staffing sales areas
  • Helping customers
  • Running checkout stations
  • Carrying larger purchases
  • Selling food and beverages
  • Packing up / tossing out remaining donations
  • Cleaning the parking lot after the sale

Volunteer shifts and specific assignments will be announced before the event. Scouts should expect this to be a hands-on service and fundraising project. Adult supervision will be required throughout the event.

How Items Will Be Priced

Most items will be priced to sell quickly. Our goal is to move a large amount of merchandise during one day, not to hold out for the highest possible price on every item.

Higher-value items may be researched and priced individually. Please notify the sale organizers when donating anything that may be valuable, collectible or unusual.

Unless specifically arranged in advance, all donated items become the property of Troop 79 and will not be returned after the sale.

What Happens to Unsold Items?

We will work with local charitable organizations -- Goodwill, St. Vincent, and ReStore -- to donate as many acceptable leftovers as possible. Different charities accept different types of merchandise, and they may reject items that are damaged, dirty, incomplete or not suitable for resale. Some remaining items may need to be recycled or discarded.

Careful screening before the sale will help us minimize waste and disposal costs.

How We Can Reach Our Goal

A successful sale will require more than a few families cleaning out their basements. We need the entire troop to participate.

If every Scout family:

  • Donates several boxes of good merchandise
  • Finds additional donations from friends and neighbors
  • Promotes the sale to their community
  • Volunteers before, during and after the event

we will have the inventory, attendance and workforce needed to make this one of Troop 79's largest fundraisers.

Save the Date - August 29th

Additional information about donation dates, volunteer shifts, item drop-off and sale hours will be announced soon.
